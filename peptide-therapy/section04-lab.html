AThree up tile area ×1.9 · four columns to three ships as-is

The cheapest honest answer to make them bigger, and the only one here that adds nothing to the page at all — three columns instead of four, a taller floor, the mark up from 58px to 80, and the one-line descriptor comes out of the popup onto the face of the tile. That last part is what the extra room actually buys: today a reader has to open a tile to learn anything beyond its name, and eight names alone is a menu rather than a question. ⚠️ It leaves a hole. Eight tiles in three columns is 3 + 3 + 2, so the bottom right is empty. Two ways out and both are yours: a ninth tile — “not sure yet, book the ten-minute call”, which is new copy and a new route — or keep four columns and take only the height, which is this variant with one line deleted. Stretching the eighth tile across the gap is the one thing I would not do: it hands Sexual Health twice the weight of the other seven, and the section’s own lede says they are eight goals of equal standing.

BThe dark room ground #2E2228 · name 12.5:1 · mark 5.4:1 re-opens round 17

The new colour, and the biggest change in feel for the least code — eight tokens move and nothing else does. The section becomes a lit room: an ink ground with a soft plum glow behind the grid, tiles as warm dark objects, and the gold marks finally reading as metal rather than as ink drawings. It is the register the chain scene already occupies, so the page would run dark through the scene and stay dark through the choosing, then come back up to the dawn at the doctors. ⚠️ Read this part before replying. Eight days ago you picked letter D — burgundy for the chosen tile. Burgundy against this ground is 46/255 at its widest channel: a chosen tile would be a shadow. So on B the lit tile has to become gold with ink type, which is letter A from that lab. Picking B un-picks D. That is not an objection — gold on ink is the more beautiful of the two — but it should be a decision rather than a side effect. ⚠️ It also costs the alternation: the page currently runs dawn · ivory · dawn · ivory · dawn, and an ink chapter is a third ground. Cheap to fix, but it is a fix.

CThe gallery 3:4 plates · tile area ×1.5 · mark at 230px the eight marks need re-drawing

Eight tall plates, and the mark stops being a badge in the corner and becomes the plate itself — a watermark at 230px with the name across the foot, a gold rule that runs the full width as you approach. It is the most expensive-looking option here and the one that reads least like a form. ⚠️ Its price is honest work, not a token. These are the same eight SVGs at the same 1.3 stroke, scaled four times up, so the line thins to a quarter of the weight it was drawn at. It survives because a watermark is allowed to be faint — but if you pick C, the eight marks want re-drawing at plate weight, and that is a real piece of drawing, not a detail. The other thing to watch is length: eight 3:4 plates make section 04 roughly a screen and a half taller, which is a lot of scrolling before the doctors.

DThe mark draws itself ~40 lines · no dependency · 0 KB ships as-is

Each mark strokes itself on as its tile arrives, bond first and then the beads, staggered across the grid — and re-draws when you hover it. It is the page’s own chain scene spoken at tile scale, which is why it feels made rather than applied: the hero spends twelve seconds writing a peptide, and then eight small ones write themselves in the section below it. The whole thing is getTotalLength() written into a CSS variable; there is no library and nothing to download. ⚠️ The filled beads cannot dash — a circle with a fill has no stroke to offset — so they fade and scale in on a delay instead, which is also what makes it read as bead-after-bond rather than one line unspooling. Of everything here this is the highest ratio of feels alive to costs anything, and it combines with A, B or C without a single conflict.

EThe lens tilt 4.5° · mark at 46px of Z · pointer only ships as-is

The card becomes a physical thing on a table: a soft gloss travels under the pointer, the tile tilts up to four and a half degrees away from your hand, and the mark and the name ride forward in front of the surface. This is the direct answer to something interesting to click — the tile answers before the click, which is what makes a reader try one. ⚠️ 4.5° is a ceiling, not a starting point. Past about six degrees a card stops reading as an object on a table and starts reading as a web component, which is the exact register this page’s restraint is buying distance from. ⚠️ It is pointer-only and nothing depends on it — a phone gets the base tile, untouched. That is a feature: it can never be the thing that makes the grid legible. Combines with everything except F, which wants the same Z axis.

FThe turn 750ms · the popup, in place the dialog may become redundant

The + turns the tile over. On the back are that goal’s own outcomes and a Choose control, and you turn it back with the same +. Curiosity made literal — the reader is shown that a tile has a back, which is a much stronger invitation than a small circle that opens an overlay. It also keeps them inside the grid: no dimming, no scroll lock, nothing to close. ⚠️ The select surface and the flip cannot both own the card. Round 12 already fought this — the tile chooses, the + opens — and here the answer is that a turned tile switches its select surface off and the back carries its own Choose. A click on a face never means two things. ⚠️ The real question F asks is about the dialog: if the back of the tile carries the outcomes, what is the popup still for? Probably the two paragraphs and the doctor route — but that is a decision about the section, not about an animation, and it should be made deliberately rather than discovered later.

GThe vial three.js r166 · one canvas · 8 scissor viewports +682 KB · a runtime the page does not have

Real 3D on the cards, and the version of it that does not throw your eight marks away. Each tile gets a small glass vial turning slowly in studio light, and suspended inside it is that tile’s own mark — the shield, the head, the coil — rendered from the same SVG the flat tile uses. A peptide clinic showing your goal in a vial is a better idea than a generic molecule, and it is the reason to build it this way: the first instinct is to replace the marks with eight rotating peptide chains, and eight rotating peptide chains say “peptide” eight times and say nothing about auto-immunity or sleep. ⚠️ It is one canvas, not eight. A single renderer draws eight scissored viewports of about 90px each, so the cost is one WebGL context and roughly a thousand triangles, not eight of anything. ⚠️ The dependency is the real price — 682 KB of vendored three.js on a page that has carried no 3D runtime since round 10 removed the silk hero. It is a vendor import, never a CDN importmap. ⚠️ And it must fall back silently: with no WebGL context the flat mark simply stays, which is the shipped tile exactly — the plate is kept underneath for precisely that reason, so nothing moves when the 3D is absent.

HThe frosted field one scene · tiles at 58% alpha · blur 17px breaks a measured invariant

The Spline look, built with the tools we already own. One continuous peptide chain drifts behind the whole grid, and the tiles become frosted panes over it — the chain reads softly through every card and sharpens where you hover, because the hovered tile drops its blur. It is the most modern-feeling thing in this lab and the one that would date fastest. ⚠️⚠️ It breaks something that is currently measured. The harness asserts that a tile and its ground stay at least 8/255 apart, which is the one number keeping the eight reading as objects rather than as ruled-off regions of the page. A translucent tile has no fixed colour, so that number becomes whatever the chain happens to be doing behind it at that instant. Adopting H means replacing that assertion with a rendered-pixel sample, not deleting it — an invariant that stops being checkable is an invariant that stops existing. ⚠️ And the frames are real: eight simultaneous backdrop-filter layers over a live canvas is the most expensive thing either page would be doing, on the device class least able to afford it.

IOn Spline a written answer, not a mock-up the first externally-hosted runtime

Spline is a hosting decision wearing a design decision’s clothes, so it is answered here rather than mocked up. A Spline scene on this page means three new things at once: the @splinetool/runtime player fetched from a CDN, a .splinecode scene file served from Spline’s own hosts, and an editor seat for whoever will keep the scene up to date. Every one of those is a dependency on somebody else’s uptime for a section that is currently a single self-contained HTML file with zero build. ⚠️ It also would not run in our own labs, which are opened as artifacts under a policy that blocks every external host — so a Spline variant could be shown to you in a browser tab and nowhere else in this workflow. G and H are the vendored answers to what Spline is usually wanted for: G is the glossy object on the card, H is the ambient 3D field behind the layout, and both live in this repository with the licence recorded. If you want Spline itself anyway, it is perfectly doable — send the scene link or the exported .splinecode, and it becomes about ninety lines plus a decision about what shows while it loads. It is not a technical objection; it is the bill, stated before rather than after.
