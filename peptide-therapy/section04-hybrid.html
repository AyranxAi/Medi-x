Eight things worth knowing. Seven are decided in the build above and can be reversed in a line; one still needs a word from you.

Decided — reversible

Your eight, in your order

The photographs arrived as eight files named by the tool that made them, and they map 1:1 onto the eight goals in the order the page already lists them — antibodies, neurons, villi, lipid droplets, follicles, muscle fibre, DNA, neurovascular. They are renamed to images/pathways/<goal>-master.png and the cards eat a webp encoded from them: 1.9 MB of PNG became 95 KB of card, eight of them. The masters are renamed, never re-encoded — they are what to go back to if a crop or a size ever changes.

Decided — reversible

The + turns the card

Your addition, and it earns its place: the front stays a photograph with a name and one line, and everything else — the full description and the outcomes — is on the back. It is only on the card in front. A + on a 110px card at the far end of the ring is a target nobody can hit and a decoration everybody can see. While a card is turned its face stops taking clicks, so a click never means two things at once.

Fixed — your catch

The ring turns the right way now

You were right, and it was a real error. On a ring seen from above the point nearest you is the lowest on screen and the ring climbs away as it recedes. I had it inverted — the front card sat highest and the outer cards dipped below it, which is a hill with a valley in the middle, not a circle. Now every card’s foot follows the ellipse: the front one is lowest and each card rises 52px as it goes round. Near is properly bigger too. It always shrank, but by a flat 5% a step, which the eye reads as nothing; size now comes off the same arc as the height, so it falls slowly near the front and quickly at the edges — 0.98, 0.91, 0.80, 0.66 — which is what perspective actually does.

Decided — your pick

Wide, with the crop stated

Wide is the default now, on your call. One thing to have on the record rather than discover later: your photographs are 4:5 portrait, so a landscape card shows roughly the middle half of each one and the rest is cropped away. I have taken the card from 338px to 372 tall, which is as landscape as it can stay beside a ring of portrait cards and buys back about a tenth of every picture for nothing. Tall is still one click away under the ring — it throws none of the image away and gives the arc a hundred more pixels — but wide is what ships unless you say otherwise. If you ever re-generate the eight, ask for 3:2 landscape masters and this cost disappears entirely.

Decided — reversible

Three one side, four the other

Eight cards and one at the front leaves seven, so the arc is 3 + front + 4 — it always was, but the fourth was so overlapped it read as hidden rather than placed. With the portrait card it is a legible card again. The asymmetry itself is not fixable, only movable: seven does not halve. Your own first reference has the same 3/4 split. Say the word if you would rather the four sat on the left.

Decided — reversible

All eight, always

A ring answers what am I looking at. This section’s reader is asking what have I picked — it builds a programme, and most people choose two or three. So no pathway is ever hidden behind another, the chosen tick holds a constant size whatever the card’s scale, and the dots below carry the chosen state as well as the position. At the far end of the ring a card is about 110px wide; the dot is the thing you can actually count.

With the description now behind the +, explore would name the wrong control — the + explores, the button commits.
