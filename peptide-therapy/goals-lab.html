The comparison

One question, eight ways of asking it.

Same eight names, same multi-select, same copy. Views 1 to 7 are literally the same eight buttons re-laid, so nothing below is a difference in content — it is a difference in ceremony, and ceremony is what tells a customer how big a decision they are making.

1 · Grid

Buys. The whole set in one glance, all your picks lit at once, and the count is obvious — eight, not thirty. Cheapest to scan.

Costs. Reads as a form. On a phone it is two columns and four rows deep, so Continue falls below the fold.

2 · Index

Buys. The only view with room for the line that says what a goal actually covers — and nobody knows whether "Musculoskeletal Injury" means their shoulder. It is also the page's own grammar: the FAQ and the seven steps are already ruled rows.

Costs. Tallest of the six. It is a reading experience, not a glance.

3 · Type

Buys. The most memorable, and the only one that feels like a publication rather than software. Choosing reads as language.

Costs. Nothing is drawn around a word, so its only affordance is the hover — and a phone has no hover. Highest risk that a first-time visitor does not realise it is a control.

4 · Shelf

Buys. Compact at any width, and the cut-off fourth tile makes the set read as continuing. Familiar thumb gesture.

Costs. Your picks scroll out of view. That is why the answer line below it is load-bearing here and merely pleasant everywhere else — remove it and a customer cannot see their own answer. It also hides the count.

5 · Pills

Buys. Fastest and smallest. All eight and the Continue fit one phone screen with room to spare.

Costs. It makes an AED 1,150 decision look exactly as light as ticking a symptom. The form is telling the customer this does not matter much.

6 · Plates

Buys. The fastest recognition of the eight and the most premium surface in the set. It is also the pattern every visitor already knows from every other health site, which is worth something.

Costs. Eight photographs that do not exist — and the harder half is that a photograph of "Auto Immune Disease" is not a thing. Photography works for products and places, not for abstract clinical categories: every answer is either a sad person or a lifestyle shot that means nothing, which is precisely how wellness sites end up looking identical.

7 · Marks

Buys. Everything the tiles were wanted for — visual, scannable, obviously clickable — with nothing to commission. The vocabulary is the page's own: beads and bonds, the peptide chain from the scene, arranged eight ways. It is truthful rather than decorative, because peptides are what is being sold.

Costs. A drawn figure takes a beat longer to read than a photograph, and eight of them is a small system somebody has to keep consistent when a ninth service appears.

8 · Symptom-first

Buys. The strongest of the six as a product. The customer never has to know which programme they need — they describe themselves and are told. That is a clinic listening, not a shop's department list.

Costs. It needs a real symptom-to-goal mapping, which is clinical and is not mine to write. The one in this lab is invented.

He asked for clickable image tiles. The answer is 7 · Marks, not 6 · Plates — and the eight stand-ins above are the argument. They are real pictures from this site's own plates, in the right register, competently cropped, and they still tell a visitor nothing about which of the eight is theirs. That is not a failure of these particular photographs. There is no photograph of Auto Immune Disease. Marks give the same tile, the same glance, the same click, and they can say something abstract because a drawing is allowed to be abstract.

2 · Index remains the most informative, because the descriptor line is the only thing on this page that answers "is that one mine?" — and the obvious combination has not been built yet: the index rows with the marks standing where the numerals are. That is one CSS rule away and it is probably the answer.

Type is the one to reach for if this section should be the page's signature moment rather than its workhorse. Pills is the wrong ceremony for an AED 1,150 decision.

The tray changed the arithmetic for the compact views. With three goals chosen, the layouts themselves show 3 of 3 — except the shelf, which shows 1, and the index, which shows 2 because eight ruled rows do not fit a laptop screen. Neither is a defect any more: the tray names all three whatever the layout hides, which is exactly why it is up from the first pick and not saved for the end. It is also the only forward control on the screen — there is no Continue, because a Continue would open the same panel forty pixels away and, in the full flow, be a second forward path competing with the sequence it sits inside.

8 is where this should end up. Ask Dr. Khalid for the mapping — which symptoms point at which of the eight — and the category picker disappears entirely.

What is still open

  • The eight descriptors are draft and are mine, not his. They touch clinical territory and need sign-off alongside the FAQ and the service tiles. Only the index view shows them; picking any other view makes the question moot.
  • View 6's mapping is invented and must not ship. It exists to make the interaction demonstrable. The shape is the proposal; the routing is his doctors' to write.
  • View 6's eight pictures are stand-ins and every tile says so. They are cropped from this site's own pathway and door plates so the layout can be judged against real imagery — the assignments are arbitrary and a functional-medicine plate does not belong on Auto Immune Disease.
  • If plates are commissioned, the face rule is not optional. LICENSES/AI-DoorPlates.txt already records it: no identifiable person, because a recognisable face on a clinic page is an implied patient testimonial whether or not any words accompany it. Worth knowing that the automatic crop used to build these stand-ins found a face in three of eight on its first pass — the rule needs a human checking it, not a setting.
  • Eight new plates also need a provenance line each, in the same file and the same form as the three door plates: origin, rights, whether a person appears. That is the process this repo already runs; it is not overhead invented here.
  • Whichever wins has to survive being the phone's first screen — the goals, the question and the Continue in one viewport at 390. Only pills and index manage it today.
