The Bond Lab

Round seven auditioned fourteen materials — chrome, glass, silk, foam — and D · Peptide bonds answered the question it was really asking: the hero is made of peptides. Round ten's first pass then offered six variations that were all the same technique, which is six answers to a question already settled.

So the axis here is how a plate is made, because that is what changes what it can say. Thirteen grounds in three chapters — flat drawings, real geometry in a real camera, and surfaces solved pixel by pixel — running from a single bond at macro range to a volume with hundreds of chains in it. D sits at the top as the reference, because it is on the page right now.

Accent

The accent question

Plate D's chains are literally gold — rgba(194,160,94) — and that is how it was picked and how it shipped. But round 9 remapped this page's --gold token to steel #7C93A8, so those chains are now the only true gold on the page. The bar above shows both readings across all thirteen plates. Gold is the brand's voice and the warmest thing left on a cold page; steel is consistent and disappears into the grade. Neither is obviously right, which is why it is a bar and not a decision — and it is the same question the grade lab's chapter C left open for the chain scene.

What each chapter costs

I · Drawn is Canvas 2D: no dependency, no WebGL, works everywhere, and cheapest on a phone. II · Built needs a WebGL context and the three.js library — the hero carried three.js until round 10 removed it, so promoting one of these means bringing it back, from vendor/ rather than a CDN. III · Rendered needs WebGL but no library: the raymarched plate is one fragment shader. Every plate falls back to a static glacier wash if its context cannot be created.

How to read the plates

Every one carries the real hero copy at the real sizes over the real glacier grade — the multiply is a layer here exactly as it is baked into the shipped draw() — so what you judge is what the page would be. Scroll a plate fully into view before judging it: the canvases only run while they are on screen, and the copy plays its entrance once. Chain and element counts fall on narrow viewports on every plate, the same way they do on the shipped page.

If one of these wins

It replaces D in peptide-therapy/index.html, one engine for another, and D joins E and Aero in the lab. Nothing else about the page moves.
