What it does, what it costs, and what is still yours

Five things worth knowing before you say yes. Three are already decided in the build above and can be reversed in a line; two need a word from you before this can ship.

Decided — reversible

All eight, always

A carousel answers what am I looking at. This section’s reader is asking what have I picked — it is a multi-select that builds a programme, and most people choose two or three. So no pathway is ever hidden behind another, and the chosen tick is drawn at a constant size on every card whatever that card's scale, so counting your choices costs a glance rather than a squint. That one rule is what lets the orbit and the multi-select live together.

Decided — reversible

It says add, not explore

Both boards put Explore or Discover pathway on the card. The pill here is the page’s own, character for character: Add to your programme. Round 12 moved this section off browsing and onto building, and the wording is the whole difference — a section that says explore is a brochure, this one assembles something you then pay for.

Decided — reversible

No 01–08

The editorial board numbers the cards. Numerals were taken off these tiles deliberately: on a list you pick several things from, a number implies an order that does not exist, and someone who chooses the third and the seventh is left wondering about the ones between. The mark identifies the card instead. Say the word and they come back — it is one line.

Open — needs you

The artwork

Your second board is roughly two-thirds photograph per card, and those eight photographs do not exist — the section has eight drawn marks and no imagery. So the focused card carries a glass vessel with that pathway’s own mark suspended inside it, in real light, turning. Eight generic molecules would say “peptide” eight times and nothing about auto-immunity or sleep. If you would rather have photography, send it — the plate takes a picture instead and the layout does not move.

Open — needs you

Spline, or this

The 3D here is three.js, already vendored in the repository under its licence — nothing is fetched from anyone else’s server. Spline would add three dependencies at once: a player from a CDN, a scene file on Spline’s hosts, and an editor seat to keep it current, on a page that is one self-contained file with no build step. Perfectly doable if you want it — send the scene and it is about ninety lines — but that is the bill, stated before rather than after.

Decided — reversible

The phone has no arc

Below 1,180px the mechanism switches off entirely and the eight become stacked editorial cards, every one open, every one a full tap target. That number is measured, not taste: eight cards fanned either side of the centre need about that much before the outermost falls off the edge. A cramped arc with two pathways hidden is strictly worse than a list. Nothing about understanding the section depends on the arc having existed — which is also why it is safe to have.
