Fifteen ways to show the programme

Round 11 put the client's programme copy on the page in one shape each — the seven steps as a hairline ledger, what's included and excluded as two columns, the two audiences as two opening rows. Each of those was chosen against the page's box budget on the day. None of them was chosen against an alternative.

So here are the alternatives, on the same words. Every treatment renders from one copy of the client's text, so nothing in this file can win an audition by quietly tightening a sentence. Under each is a spec chip, and the height in it is measured live at the width you are viewing — because for half of these the whole argument is how much scroll the chapter costs.

Chapter A

The seven steps

The client's numbering is load-bearing and is written out in every treatment below — never generated by a CSS counter, because steps 3 and 5 carry nested lists a counter would number against. Steps 3 and 5 are also the two that break most of these layouts: the blood panel is eleven assays and the protocol is four items with two sub-items. Judge every treatment on those two, not on step 2.

A1

The Ledger as shipped

A hairline per row, a Megante numeral in a fixed track, nothing boxed. It borrows the FAQ's rhythm, and it was chosen because by this point in the scroll the page has already spent seven cards on the tiles and two on the audiences — seven more boxes would have been the third grid in a row.

Structure
7 rows · 1 hairline each · no containers
Height here
—
Wins
Everything visible at once. No interaction to discover, nothing to fail. Prints.
Costs
Longest honest read on the page. Step 3 dominates the section by sheer mass.

A2

The Spine smallest change

A1's information with a direction added: one rule down the left, a bead per step, and the rule fills as the section scrolls. Seven separate rows become one continuous thing — which is what a programme is. The cheapest of the eight to adopt: it is a rule, a bead and a scroll listener over markup that already exists.

Structure
A1's rows · 1 rail · 7 beads · scroll-linked fill
Height here
—
Wins
Reads as a journey, not a list. Degrades to A1 exactly if the script never runs.
Costs
Adds a scroll listener to a page that already runs a pinned canvas scene.

A3

The Eight Weeks needs his dates

The only treatment that adds information rather than styling: the programme drawn on a calendar. It is also the only one this lab cannot finish alone. The client's source gives three durations and no dates — two months of protocol, four weeks of mentorship, a further four weeks of follow-up. Those three are drawn. Steps 1–4 and 6 sit in an undated band, because inventing "week 1, week 2" for them publishes a schedule the clinic never agreed to and a patient will hold them to it.

Structure
1 undated band (5 cards) · 8-week ruler · 2 bars
Height here
—
Wins
Answers "how long is this?" — the question the ledger never answers at all.
Blocked on
Dates for steps 1–4 and 6, or a decision to keep them undated.

A4

The Rail shortest

Seven cards on one horizontal scroll-snap track. The chapter stops being seven screens of scroll and becomes one card tall. Native scroll, not a JS carousel — keyboard, trackpad, touch momentum and a real scrollbar all come free, and every hand-built carousel has had to reimplement those badly. Steps 3 and 5 do not fit a card, so each card carries a précis and opens for the full text.

Structure
7 cards · x-snap · arrows + progress · in-card expand
Height here
—
Wins
By far the least vertical cost. Works the same on a phone as on a desktop.
Costs
Horizontal scroll hides content by default — some readers never find card 5.

A5

The Doors most compact

The FAQ's own accordion, applied to the seven, one open at a time. The whole chapter collapses to seven headlines and one body. It is the shortest treatment here by a wide margin — and it is the only one that hides the programme by default, which is precisely what a page selling that programme may not want.

Structure
7 rows · 1 panel open · grid-template-rows transition
Height here
—
Wins
Scannable in one glance. Material already on the page, so nothing new to learn.
Costs
Seven clicks to read the programme. Search engines index it, readers may not.

A6

The Stage most clinical

A sticky index of the seven beside one panel that swaps. Reads as a pathway rather than a list, and lets a returning reader jump straight to step 5. Below the container breakpoint it has no honest two-column form, so it falls back to A5's accordion rather than pretending — switch the width control to Phone 390 to see the fallback, which is the form most of the traffic will actually get.

Structure
Sticky 7-item index + 1 panel · A5 below 700px
Height here
—
Wins
Random access. Fixed height regardless of how long step 3 is.
Costs
Two layouts to maintain. Needs viewport height for step 3's assay panel.

A7

The Plates most editorial

The magazine reading. Each step is a spread — an outsize Megante numeral as a graphic, the body in a column beside it, sides alternating. It is the treatment that most looks like the rest of this site, and it is by a distance the tallest thing in the lab. That trade is the entire decision, and the height chip states it rather than leaving it to be discovered after launch.

Structure
7 spreads · alternating · numeral to 190px
Height here
—
Wins
Unmistakably the brand. Gives step 2 the same weight as step 3, which the ledger cannot.
Costs
Tallest of the eight. Collapses to a plain stack on a phone, losing the whole device.

A8

The Chart most authoritative

The programme drawn as the clinical document it describes: ruled rows, a reference column, tick boxes, and the assay list set the way a requisition sets one. It sounds more like the clinic than any other treatment here. It also reads coldest — and a page selling reassurance may not want its programme to look like paperwork. That is an editorial call, not a technical one.

Structure
1 ruled document · 7 rows · 3 columns · boxed assays
Height here
—
Wins
Reads as medicine. Step 3's eleven assays finally look like they belong.
Costs
Cold. Introduces a document material the page does not otherwise own.

Chapter B

What's included, what's excluded

Nine capitalised verbs on one side and two exclusions on the other — and the two sides are wildly unequal in mass, which is the design problem. Two columns give an eight-item list a two-item neighbour and a great deal of white space. Each treatment below answers that imbalance differently. ANALYSE and INTERPRET stay nested under COLLATE in all four: the source reads "COLLATE all the above, in order to / ANALYSE / INTERPRET", so they are what the collating is for.

B1

Two Columns as shipped

The plainest possible reading, and it does the one thing the section exists to do: put the two lists where they can be compared without scrolling between them. Its weakness is visible immediately at full width — the right column runs out halfway.

Structure
2 columns · 8 items vs 2 items
Height here
—
Wins
Instantly legible as a comparison. Nothing to operate.
Costs
The imbalance shows. On a phone the two columns stack and stop being a comparison at all.

B2

The Balance keeps the comparison

One ledger, two bands, and the difference carried by a shape as well as a heading — the page's ✦ for what's in, a hollow ring for what's out. Stacking rather than columning means the imbalance stops being a hole in the layout and becomes what it actually is: a long list and a short one. It is also the only treatment here that reads identically on a phone and a desktop.

Structure
1 sheet · 2 bands · ✦ / ○ marks · rules top and bottom
Height here
—
Wins
Same reading at every width. The marks survive a greyscale print.
Costs
Taller than B1 at desktop. You read the two lists in sequence, not side by side.

B3

The Verbs strongest position

The nine capitals are already the structure of the client's copy, so here they get the left track and every clause reads off them. EVALUATE · ESTABLISH · UNDERSTAND · TEST · COLLATE · CREATE · HEAL becomes a method with a shape. The exclusions drop to a quiet panel underneath — which is a commercial position, not a layout choice: it says the method is the story and the exclusions are terms. If the exclusions are load-bearing at the point of sale, this is the wrong treatment.

Structure
9 verb rows (2 nested) · 1 footnote panel
Height here
—
Wins
The verbs finally do work instead of sitting inside sentences as bold text.
Costs
Demotes the exclusions. Needs sign-off that that is intended.

B4

The Tabs smallest

One panel, two tabs, real tab semantics — roving arrow-key focus, role="tablist", the lot, because a pair of divs that swap on click is not a tab set and a screen reader will not announce it as one. Smallest footprint of the four. The catch is structural: a tab set says these two things are alternatives, and included/excluded are not alternatives, they are two halves of one answer.

Structure
2 tabs · 1 panel · roving tabindex
Height here
—
Wins
Fixed, small height. The short list no longer looks like an oversight.
Costs
Semantically wrong for a pair that should be read together. Hides half the answer.

Chapter C

For women · For men

Two teaser rows that open a dialog of roughly thirty and nineteen clinical symptoms. The question underneath is not really styling: it is whether that list belongs behind a button at all. C3 answers it by putting the whole thing in the page, and the height chip is the answer. Note this is hormone-symptom copy on a peptide page — the client supplied it for this section, and if it should instead route to the hormone page the change is two templates deleted and two hrefs added.

C1

Two Rows as shipped

The eight tiles' own material at row scale — ivory fill, hairline, 2px radius, the circled + that the dialog answers. Deliberately not a new material: the tiles already taught the reader that this shape opens, and a new one would have to teach it again for two more controls.

Structure
2 rows · stretched hit area · dialog on open
Height here
—
Wins
Tiny. Keeps a 30-item symptom list out of the middle of the chapter.
Costs
Two more boxes in a chapter that already has boxes. Gives the audiences no visual weight.

C2

The Diptych no new assets

Two plates instead of two rows, using the landing page's own doors — menopause-941 and trt-941 already ship in this repo, so this costs no new photography, no new encode and no new licence. It gives the two audiences the weight a clinic page usually gives them, and it is the only treatment here that puts a human being in the chapter.

Structure
2 plates 4:3 · gradient scrim · caption at foot
Height here
—
Wins
Weight and warmth. Reuses assets already encoded and paid for.
Blocked on
Scrim alphas are copied from the doors and not yet measured against these crops.

C3

The Switch answers the real question

No dialog at all: a two-way control and the full list standing in the page. This is the treatment that tests the assumption behind the other two — that the symptom lists are too long to show. Read the height chip before arguing about the aesthetics. It is also the only version whose list is on the page for a search engine to index and for a reader to Ctrl-F.

Structure
1 switch · 1 panel · full list in 2 columns
Height here
—
Wins
Indexable, findable, printable. No dialog code at all.
Costs
Puts a wall of clinical symptoms in the middle of a chapter about how the programme runs.

How to read this

Three decisions, not fifteen

Pick one from each chapter. They are independent — any A works with any B with any C — but two combinations are worth avoiding, and both are about repetition rather than taste: A4 with C2 puts a horizontal card track directly above two photographic doors, which is three interactive surfaces in one chapter; and A5 with B4 and C1 makes every single thing in the section something you have to open before you can read it.

Two of the fifteen cannot ship on this lab's say-so alone:

  1. A3 · The Eight Weeks needs the client to date steps 1–4 and 6, or to confirm they stay undated. The three durations it draws are the only ones his copy contains.
  2. C2 · The Diptych needs its scrim re-measured against those two plates at those two crops — render, hide the copy, sample the worst 2% of the ground behind it. The alphas in this file are the doors' geometry as a starting point, and are not a measurement.

Everything else here is adoptable as it stands: the markup is static in every case, and the copy is already the string that ships. Do not port this file's renderer — it exists so fifteen treatments could share one copy of the client's text, and whichever treatment is chosen gets hand-written into index.html as static markup, exactly as A1 already is.
