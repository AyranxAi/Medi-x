Flushes usually do fade with time. The tissue changes don't. Bone loss is fastest in the years right after menopause and it is silent — there is no symptom to tell you it is happening — and the same goes for the cardiovascular and cognitive shifts of this stage.

Bioidentical hormones are chemically identical to hormones naturally produced by our bodies. For many women, regulated bioidentical hormone therapy can be an appropriate option — but hormone therapy is not one-size-fits-all. Safety depends on your medical history, age, individual risk factors, the type of hormone, dose and how it is administered.

Ideally for the rest of your life.

There is no fixed time limit for hormone therapy.

How long you stay on BHRT depends on your individual health profile, your life goals, and how you respond to treatment.

Some women use hormone therapy for a few years, while others may choose to continue for much longer under appropriate medical supervision. Current guidance does not support an arbitrary cut-off simply because a woman reaches a certain age or has been on treatment for a certain number of years.

The important thing is regular review. Your treatment, dose, benefits and individual risk factors should be reassessed at least once every 6 months, to make sure the therapy continues to be appropriate for you.

At Medi-Gyn, we don't treat hormone therapy as a fixed-duration protocol — we personalise it as your body and needs change.

BHRT can be delivered in several different ways depending on the hormone and the individual woman's needs.

Estrogen may be prescribed as a cream, patch, gel (systemic therapy) or vaginal treatment (non-systemic therapy).

Progesterone is commonly taken orally.

Testosterone may be prescribed through the skin in a cream — transdermally — when clinically appropriate.

Pregnenolone and DHEA are in a capsule form.

There isn't one standard BHRT protocol for every woman. The hormone, dose and route of administration should be selected according to her symptoms, medical history, stage of menopause and individual risk factors.
